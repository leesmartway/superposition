3. Using the superposition theorem, find V3.

Press Start to work the problem one step at a time. Back and Next move between steps, and the arrow keys do the same. The three ground symbols in the printed figure are one common wire, drawn here as the bottom rail.

1Remove one voltage source (15 V)

Superposition looks at one source at a time. Take the 15 V source out first and put a plain wire where it was. A voltage source turned down to zero volts is a short.

V2 replaced by a short (0 Ω)

Only the 5 V source is left

2Indicate the current directions

Current leaves the + terminal of the 5 V source, goes through R1, and splits at the node. Part goes down through R3. The rest goes on through R2 and the short. Mark + on the end of each resistor where the current enters.

I1 and I2 flow to the right

I3 flows down

2Total resistance and current

R3 and R2 both run from the node to ground, so they are in parallel. R1 is in series with that pair.

VT = 5 V

REQ = 1.8 kΩ ∥ 1 kΩ = 642.857 Ω

RT = 1.5 kΩ + REQ = 2142.857 Ω

IT = 5 V ÷ 2142.857 Ω

∴ IT = I1 = 2.333 mA

2Current divider

The 2.333 mA splits between R2 and R3. With only two resistors, each branch gets IT times the opposite resistor, divided by the two resistors added together.

1 kΩ + 1.8 kΩ = 2.8 kΩ

I2 = 2.333 mA × 1.8 kΩ ÷ 2.8 kΩ

= 1.5 mA

I3 = 2.333 mA × 1 kΩ ÷ 2.8 kΩ

= 0.833 mA

Check: 1.5 mA + 0.833 mA = 2.333 mA, the current that came in through R1.

3Remove the other voltage source (5 V)

Put the 15 V source back and short the 5 V source instead. This part is written in green so the two sources stay easy to tell apart. Current now leaves the + terminal of the 15 V source, goes left through R2, and splits at the node between R3 and R1.

V1 replaced by a short (0 Ω)

I2 and I1 flow to the left

I3 still flows down

3Total resistance and current

This time R1 and R3 are the parallel pair, and R2 is in series with them.

VT = 15 V

REQ = 1.5 kΩ ∥ 1.8 kΩ = 818.18 Ω

RT = 1 kΩ + REQ = 1818.18 Ω

IT = 15 V ÷ 1818.18 Ω

∴ IT = I2 = 8.25 mA

3Current divider

The 8.25 mA splits between R1 and R3. Same rule: the opposite resistor on top, the two added together on the bottom.

1.5 kΩ + 1.8 kΩ = 3.3 kΩ

I1 = 8.25 mA × 1.8 kΩ ÷ 3.3 kΩ

= 4.5 mA

I3 = 8.25 mA × 1.5 kΩ ÷ 3.3 kΩ

= 3.75 mA

Check: 4.5 mA + 3.75 mA = 8.25 mA, the current that came in through R2.

4Compare the current directions

Put both results on the one circuit, blue from the 5 V source and green from the 15 V source, and check each resistor. Opposite directions subtract, and the larger current decides which way it really flows. Same directions add.

I1, I2: opposite, so subtract

I1 = 4.5 − 2.333 = 2.167 mA

I2 = 8.25 − 1.5 = 6.75 mA

I3: same direction, so add

In R1 and R2 the green current is larger, so both really flow to the left.

4Voltage across R3

The question asks for V3, so I3 is the one that matters. Both sources push current down through R3, so add the two parts and then use Ohm's law.

I3 = 0.833 mA + 3.75 mA

= 4.583 mA

V3 = 4.583 mA × 1.8 kΩ

V3 = 8.25 V

Check from the other side: R2 drops 6.75 mA × 1 kΩ = 6.75 V, and 15 V − 6.75 V = 8.25 V at the node. Same answer.
